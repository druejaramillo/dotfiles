# PDF Exploration

1. Identify the document and version and confirm access. Use the available PDF parsing tools, or `pdftotext` (installed on this machine) to extract text. For long files, extract/read bounded page ranges rather than dumping the entire file into context. Do not treat a truncated extraction as complete coverage.
2. Map abstract, methods, results, tables, figures, captions, citations, and supplement references. Follow cross-references across pages before accepting a metric or claimed causal result.
3. When citing a quantitative or disputed claim, record the exact page/table/figure or quoted passage. Cross-check figure captions against methods, units, sample size, and whether a figure shows real or illustrative data.
4. If extraction drops columns, equations, or figure labels, inspect the rendered page when a viewer/screenshot tool is available; otherwise note the check is blocked. Scanned or protected PDFs may require OCR or access you do not have; never reconstruct unseen text from memory.
5. Store short notes with page locators for large investigations when useful. A single-PDF summary should describe the source's claims, not present them as independently validated facts.
