# Literature review

Map a bounded scholarly topic, lab, author or publication corpus.

1. Define scope, period and coverage. Resolve an author's or lab's identity
   before attributing publications. Read the scholarly-source reference.
2. Search across terms and source families, not one ranking. Identify canonical
   and recent work, methodological families, independent replications,
   disagreement and open questions.
3. Read methods/results for papers carrying the conclusion. Cross-check PDF
   figures/tables where needed. Distinguish preprints and reviewed versions.
4. Synthesize meaningful research trajectories and why selected work changed
   direction. Prestige and citation counts do not establish correctness.
5. Check the cited identities, versions and pivotal results. State missing
   records, access limits and how representative the reachable corpus is.

Use the requested location or `outputs/<slug>-lit.md`. Include scope/method,
a source-linked map with year and stable identifier, agreement/disagreement,
evidence limitations and next reading or testable questions. Large corpora may
benefit from notes; neither notes nor a plan-approval ceremony are required.
