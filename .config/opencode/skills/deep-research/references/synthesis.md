# Synthesis And Adversarial Review

Synthesis is not compression. Build it in this order:

1. List the key claims and map their independent supporting source types.
2. Identify convergence, divergence, silence, and trend patterns.
3. Convert each material fact into an insight: fact, context, implication.
4. Apply the so-what test: state which decision, risk, or opportunity changes.
5. Red-team the emerging conclusion. Steelman the opposing case; check
   survivorship, recency, source, and incentive bias; test base rates and ask
   what would make the conclusion fail.
6. Calibrate confidence: high for three or more independent source types,
   medium for two or one highly authoritative source, low for single-source or
   materially incomplete evidence, speculative for an explicit inference.
7. Report material absences: missing data, voices, comparisons, or timelines,
   why they matter, and the concrete action needed to close each gap.

The final narrative should lead with the answer, provide the landscape needed to
understand it, develop the strongest evidence, introduce the counterargument,
then resolve the reader's options and next steps.
